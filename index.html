Uncompromising Safety

Expedition Infrastructure & Safety Pillars

Climbing high mountains requires careful preparation and teamwork. Our team sets up warm camps, safety ropes, and emergency backup before anyone starts the climb.

High-Altitude Operational Safety Architecture

Safety comes first on all our trips. We follow standard mountain rules from the UIAA climbing federation. Our guides check your oxygen levels daily, monitor storm forecasts, and turn back early if bad weather moves in.

UIAA Medical Commission Compliance
Climbers must have an oxygen reading above 75% at Camp 3. Guides test finger levels twice each morning before letting anyone move higher up the mountain.
Pre-Push Medical Diagnostics
Our camp doctor checks your pulse and oxygen every morning. Climbers need medical sign-off at Base Camp before heading above 7,000 meters.
Autonomous Reconnaissance & Aerial Rescue
We fly camera drones early in the morning to find safe paths through moving glacier ice. Rescue helicopters also stay on standby at nearby airports in case of emergencies.
Sherpa Porter Welfare & Ethical Load Caps
Porters carry a strict maximum weight of 20kg. Every helper receives the same warm boots, down jackets, and medical insurance coverage as lead guides.
24/7 Base Dispatch

Aerial Evacuation & Satellite Ops

High-altitude rescue helicopters stay ready at mountain airfields. Our expedition leaders carry satellite messengers to send live updates back to our base desk in Kathmandu.
